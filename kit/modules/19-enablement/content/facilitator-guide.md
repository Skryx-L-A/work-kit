# Facilitator guide

For anyone who runs a session from this pack: the program owner, an AI champion, a team lead.
You do not need to be an AI expert. You need to know the company rules, have tried every
exercise yourself, and be willing to say "I don't know, I will find out".

## Two weeks before

- [ ] Pick the session: `art4-literacy-session.md` or one session from `curriculum.md`.
- [ ] Check that every `TODO(ask IT)` in the material you will show is filled in. If a value
      is still unknown, say so in the session and teach the strict default.
- [ ] Confirm the tool participants will use is approved for the data in the exercises.
      Prefer exercises with PUBLIC or synthetic data.
- [ ] Book 90 minutes for the baseline session, 60 minutes for a track session. Groups of
      6 to 12 work best; above 15, add a second facilitator.
- [ ] Ask participants in the invitation for one task from their own work that repeats every
      week and takes more than 15 minutes. Collect answers; they become your examples.
- [ ] Do every exercise yourself with the approved tool. Note where it failed: you will show
      that too.

## Day before

- [ ] Accounts and access work for every participant (a test login, not an assumption).
- [ ] Screen sharing works; font size readable from the back.
- [ ] Printed or shared: `handouts/data-classes-card.md`, `handouts/review-checklist-card.md`,
      `handouts/starter-prompt-pack.md` (only the relevant role section).
- [ ] Attendance list from `templates/literacy-record.md` prepared.

## During the session

Structure every session the same way so people know what to expect:

1. **Why (5 min).** One concrete task from the invitation answers, not a vendor story.
2. **Rules (10 min).** Data classes, human review, where to ask. Same slide every time.
3. **Show (10 to 15 min).** Live demo on a real-looking task. Include one failure: a wrong
   fact, an invented source, a confident but wrong summary. Ask the room to find it.
4. **Do (25 to 40 min).** Participants work in pairs on the exercise for their role. You walk
   around. Pairs work better than individuals: people explain to each other.
5. **Share (10 min).** Two or three pairs show one result and one problem.
6. **Next step (5 min).** One task each person will try this week, written down.

### Facilitation habits

- Ask before you tell: "What would you check in this answer before sending it?"
- Show your own prompt, including the bad first version.
- Keep the tool on the screen, not slides. Slides only for rules and structure.
- Park off-topic questions on a visible list and answer them in the follow-up mail.
- Never ask people to paste their real customer or HR data "just to see". Stop it if it
  happens, calmly, and explain why.

### Difficult moments

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| "AI will replace my job" | Take it seriously. Say what the company has said (`TODO(ask IT)`: official statement). Point to the review duty: the person stays responsible. Do not promise what you cannot know. |
| "It is useless, it made things up" | Agree it does. Show which tasks it handles well (drafting, restructuring, summarizing text you provide) and which it does not (facts it cannot see, numbers, decisions). |
| Someone is far ahead | Give them the extension task and ask them to help their neighbor. Invite them to the champion track. |
| Someone is lost with the tool itself | Pair them with a patient partner. Tool basics are not their fault. |
| A question about law or data protection you cannot answer | Write it down, name who will answer (DPO or IT), put the answer in the follow-up. Do not improvise legal answers. |
| The tool is down or slow | Switch to the paper exercise: participants write the prompt and the review checklist, the group runs the best one later. |

## Within three days after

- [ ] Send the follow-up: the prompts used, answers to parked questions, the one-page rules,
      the date of the next session.
- [ ] File the attendance record (`templates/literacy-record.md`). This record is the
      evidence that the company took AI literacy measures.
- [ ] Collect feedback (`templates/feedback-form.md`); change the material where two or more
      people struggled at the same point.
- [ ] Note which tasks participants chose; they are candidates for the use-case list.

## Adapting the material

- Keep the rules section identical across all sessions; adapt only examples and exercises.
- Replace a generic example with a team's real, cleared task whenever you can.
- When the approved tool list changes, update `handouts/data-classes-card.md` first, then
  every session that shows tools.
- Record changes at the bottom of the file you changed: date, what, why.
