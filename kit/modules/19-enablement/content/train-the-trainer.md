# Train-the-trainer: AI champions

A track for people who will run sessions in their own team and be the first contact for AI
questions there. One champion per team of about 10 to 15 people. Champions are volunteers
who use AI in their work already; they do not need to be technical.

## What a champion does

- Runs the baseline session and their role track for their team, with this pack.
- Answers everyday questions and knows when to pass one on (legal, data protection, IT).
- Collects use-case ideas and good prompts from the team and passes them on.
- Collects adoption numbers every 4 to 6 weeks (`templates/adoption-metrics.md`).
- Meets the other champions once a month for 45 minutes.

A champion does not approve tools, decide data questions, or build automations for the team
on their own. Time budget: `TODO(ask IT)`: agreed hours per month, usually 2 to 4.

## Track (three sessions of 90 minutes plus one co-delivered session)

| # | Session | Content | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | Know the material | Walk through the baseline session as a participant; then read the facilitator notes; the rules section word for word; where the pack keeps company values | Champion has done every exercise with the approved tool and noted where it failed |
| T2 | Deliver it | Each champion runs 15 minutes of the baseline session; group gives feedback on clarity, timing, and handling of a planted difficult question (see `facilitator-guide.md`) | Feedback notes; one changed example per champion |
| T3 | Beyond the session | Answering questions safely; spotting use cases; the intake route; collecting metrics; when to escalate; adapting exercises with cleared team material | Champion's own FAQ additions; first metrics plan |
| T4 | Co-delivery | Champion runs a real session with the program owner present, then a 15-minute debrief | Session delivered, record filed |

## Champion certificate

A champion is certified after T1 to T4 and one delivered session with a filed record.
Record it in `templates/literacy-record.md` with the role "champion".

## Monthly champion meeting (45 minutes)

1. Numbers: sessions run, people trained, adoption metrics (10 min).
2. What went wrong: wrong outputs, near misses, confusing rules (15 min).
3. Best new prompts and use cases; decide which go into the shared library (15 min).
4. Material changes to make, with an owner (5 min).
